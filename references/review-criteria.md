# Twenty-point manuscript review

## Tier 1

1. Characterization and character arcs — distinct, dimensional characters who change.
2. Narrative engine — the question, problem, danger, or desire driving the reader forward.
3. Structure and escalation — rising complications leading to a satisfying climax and resolution.
4. Pacing and momentum — the right speed, with breathing room but no dead weight.
5. Causality and plot logic — events follow from choices and understandable consequences.
6. Stakes and decisions — difficult choices with meaningful costs.
7. Emotional payoff — relationships, fears, hopes, sacrifices, and revelations pay off.
8. Worldbuilding and exposition — a clear, compelling world revealed organically.
9. Point of view and narrative voice — an engaging, consistent narrative perspective.
10. Ending — the central story and character arcs resolve with the intended aftertaste.

## Tier 2

11. Descriptive language — enough sensory detail to create presence without slowing the scene.
12. Dialogue — distinct voices, subtext, rhythm, and believable conversational turns.
13. Scene construction — each scene has purpose, tension, change, and an exit point.
14. Foreshadowing and setup/payoff — planted elements matter later without feeling obvious.
15. Themes and motifs — recurring ideas are developed rather than merely mentioned.
16. Humor and tonal control — jokes land without weakening danger or emotional seriousness.
17. Technical and scientific credibility — especially important for hard science fiction.
18. Continuity and consistency — chronology, physical details, knowledge, terminology, and rules.
19. Chapter openings and endings — each chapter creates curiosity or lands a satisfying turn.
20. Line-level prose — clarity, rhythm, repetition, awkward wording, grammar, and typos.

## Style decisions

Treat declared authorial choices as constraints. In particular, preserve intentional
omniscient passages and review their transitions rather than recommending their removal.
